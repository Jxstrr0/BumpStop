# Bump Stop in 3D

Plan PR 5. How the trucks are made and how the game moves them.

## The trucks are made by script

`scripts/blender/make-trucks.py` builds all three trucks with no Blender
window: run it with the Blender Python module (`pip install bpy==5.1.2`,
then `python scripts/blender/make-trucks.py`) or with Blender itself
(`blender --background --python scripts/blender/make-trucks.py`). It writes
`public/models/truck-light4x4.glb`, `truck-heavy.glb` and `truck-sxs.glb`
(about 300 KB each). The files are committed; the game's build never runs
Blender.

Everything comes from one shared kit of parts:

- **Wheel:** a rounded tire with tread blocks (so you can see it turn), a rim
  in the class's trim colour with five spokes.
- **Shock:** a graphite body with a remote reservoir, a chrome shaft, and a
  magenta coil spring (Bump Stop's highlighter colour).
- **Arms and axles:** wishbones and trailing arms for the side-by-side, solid
  axle beams with a diff for the light 4x4 and the heavy truck (plus leaf
  spring packs on the heavy truck).
- **Bits:** tubes, lamp bars, number boards, mirrors.

Each class has its own body on top: a lifted pickup with a bull bar, a roof
lamp bar and a spare in the bed (light 4x4); a cab-over rally truck with a
service box and two spare wheels (heavy truck); a tube-cage buggy with two
seats and the engine behind (side-by-side). No real brands, logos or
people.

The wheels sit where the sim's test mules have them (`cgToFront`,
`cgToRear` and `track` in `src/data/ride.ts`); `tests/models.test.ts`
checks that, and that every moving part is there. If the mules change,
change `TRUCKS` at the top of the script and run it again.

## The moving parts

Every moving part is its own named node, so the game can move it:

| Node | What it is | What the game does with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Body` | Cab, frame, cage, everything on the springs | Bounces, pitches and rolls |
| `Hub_FL` (and FR, RL, RR) | Each wheel's upright | Moves up and down with its wheel |
| `Wheel_FL` … | Wheel and tire | Spins with the distance |
| `Axle_F`, `Axle_R` | Solid axle beams | Tilt as one wheel rises past the other, taking both wheels with them |
| `Arm_FL` … | Side-by-side arms | Swing from the body to follow the wheel |
| `ShockBody_FL`, `ShockShaft_FL`, `Spring_FL` … | The shock and its spring | Stretch between `MountTop_FL` (on the body) and `MountLow_FL` (on the hub) |

`src/render/truckModel.ts` loads a model and poses it from the ride sim's
numbers: body bounce, pitch and roll, and each wheel's height, all measured
from rest, exactly as `src/sim/ride.ts` keeps them.

## Ride height

The models are drawn at the stock ride height. A build that sags less
(stiffer springs, more preload) sits higher at that end; one that sags more
sits lower. `stanceOf` in `src/ui/truckView.ts` works it out from each
axle's `sag` (`src/sim/build.ts`), and the body tilts to match.

## Where you see it

- **Build screen:** your truck on the shop floor, turning slowly (not with
  reduced motion). Drag sideways to turn it; up and down still scrolls the
  page. It only draws while it's on screen.

- **The shop (the test track):** every rig run plays in 3D at the top of
  the live view, and the break replays in slow motion under the report's
  heading. See below.

## The test track

**The road is the sim's road.** Each rig's loop is a few pieces of ground
(`src/data/rigs.ts`), and the sim builds each piece's road from a seeded
recipe (`src/sim/ground.ts`): two lines of heights, one under each wheel
track. `src/render/terrain.ts` lays those very heights out as a gravel road:
the left track, a slight crown, the right track, then shoulders, a ditch, a
bank and the forest (spruce and birch, placed by a hash so they stay put).
Every ripple, whoop, rock and jump the truck reacts to is under its wheels.
Pieces join where both are flat (each piece's ground fades out at its ends).

**Clips, not every step.** The rigs run the sim far faster than real time
(a lap in 4 seconds), so the 3D can't show every step. Instead the lap
runner and the shaker keep a short pose log (`src/sim/poses.ts`): body
bounce, pitch and roll and each wheel's height, 50 times a simulated second,
the last 4 seconds in a ring. The log only reads the sim's state; a run
with it gives exactly the same result as one without (`tests/poses.test.ts`),
and it's off for the balance bot.

`src/ui/rigView.ts` keeps asking for the newest 2.5 seconds and plays them
at true speed (`src/render/trackView.ts`), cutting to the next of five
cameras (side, front, chase, wheel, high) for each clip, like a TV feed. The
shaker's beat is slowed so it never looks faster than 2.5 a second. Dust
trails the wheels, more the faster it goes.

**The break.** When a run ends on a break, the 3D view moves under the
report's heading (the canvas keeps its picture when it moves) and replays
the last 3 seconds at 0.3× speed, closing in on the corner that broke, then
shows the break and holds it before starting again:

| Failure | What you see |
| --- | --- |
| Damper fade | Smoke curling off the shock, thicker as the oil cooks |
| Bottomed out | A jolt and a burst of dust |
| Cracked mount | The shock's top end drops off its mount |
| Snapped axle | The wheel flops out at an angle |
| Cut tire | The wheel drops onto squashed rubber |
| Cracked frame | A jolt, the camera pulled in on the whole truck |

The pace of all this is in `VIEW_3D` in `src/data/rigs.ts`.

With reduced motion the camera stays side-on: no cuts, swoops or shakes
(the truck itself still moves as the sim says).

three.js and the models only load when a 3D view first comes near the
screen (`import('../render/garage')`), so the first screen stays quick; the
models folder doesn't count towards the first-load budget
(`scripts/check-bundle.mjs`). If the phone can't do WebGL, a line says so and
the rest of the game works as before.

The colours come from the page's CSS variables (`src/render/look.ts`), so
the 3D follows the roadbook look and turns to the night-stage colours in
dark mode.
