# Bump Stop in 3D

Plan PR 5. How the trucks are made and how the game moves them.

## The trucks are made by script

`scripts/blender/make-trucks.py` builds all three trucks with no Blender
window: run it with the Blender Python module (`pip install bpy==5.1.2`,
then `python scripts/blender/make-trucks.py`) or with Blender itself
(`blender --background --python scripts/blender/make-trucks.py`). Add
`-- sxs` (or `light4x4`, `heavy`) to build just one. It writes
`public/models/truck-light4x4.glb.gz`, `truck-heavy.glb.gz` and
`truck-sxs.glb.gz`: ordinary `.glb` models, zipped. The files are committed;
the game's build never runs Blender.

**Why zipped:** the models are mostly long lists of numbers that repeat, so
zipping makes each one about a fifth of its size (about 190 KB to download
instead of about 900 KB). The phone's browser unzips it itself
(`DecompressionStream`, in `src/render/truckModel.ts`), with no extra code
to download.

Everything comes from one shared kit of parts, and each class has its own
body on top:

- **Light 4x4:** a lifted single-cab pickup with a raked windscreen, a raised
  bonnet, round headlamps in a slatted grille, a bull bar with spot lamps,
  arch flares, door lines and handles, a bed with capped rails and a
  tailgate, a sport bar with work lamps and a roof lamp bar.
- **Heavy truck:** a cab-over rally truck with a split windscreen, a sun
  visor and a roof deflector, a heavy bumper with round lamps and magenta tow
  eyes, cab steps, big mirrors, a service box with roller shutters, a ladder
  up the back, amber sand ladders, fuel tanks and mud flaps.
- **Side-by-side:** a tube cage with gussets at the joints, two seats with
  headrests and magenta harnesses, half doors and window nets, a nose with a
  tube bumper, black fenders, a rear deck over the engine and a whip with a
  magenta flag.

No real brands, logos or people. Hard edges get a small bevel so they catch
the light like pressed metal.

## The parts you fit show on the truck

Each model carries every part a player can bolt on, and the game shows only
the build's own (`fit()` in `src/render/truckModel.ts`). Swap a part on the
build screen and the truck on the shop floor changes at once; the truck on
the test track and in the raids is the one you built.

| Slot | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Dampers | Twin-tube: plain black. Monotube: silver. Remote reservoir: blue end caps and a separate can on a hose. Bypass: a fat silver body with three outside tubes and blue adjusters. Internal bypass: black with a piggyback can. Electronic: black with an amber valve box and a wire |
| Springs | Coil-sprung trucks show magenta coils and the axle's links; leaf-sprung ones show leaf packs (the top leaf is magenta) and no coils |
| Bump stops | Rubber: a black cone. Urethane: an amber stack. Hydraulic: a small silver can with a blue cap |
| Anti-roll bars | None, a plain black bar, or a bar with flat blue blade arms |
| Axles | Standard: a plain diff cover. Heavy-duty: a sleeved housing, a truss and a finned cover. Chromoly: a blue cover and chrome hub flanges. On the side-by-side: thicker drive shafts, or chrome shafts with blue boots |
| Wheels | Steel: a birch-white dish with round holes. Alloy: silver spokes. Beadlocks: black with a bright bolted ring |
| Tires | All-terrain: fine shallow blocks. Rally: big square blocks. Heavy ply: chunky lugs that climb a fat sidewall. All the same size, so a swap never moves the truck |
| Guards | Skid plates under the nose and gearbox; full armour adds rock sliders, diff guards and tank guards |
| Spares | Nothing, one spare wheel and a crate, or two spares, more crates and red jerry cans |
| Engine | The bits you see outside: the V8's bonnet scoop and twin pipes, the diesels' snorkel, the turbo diesel's intercooler; the heavy truck's exhaust stacks and air intake; the side-by-side's pipes, turbo and air box |
| Frame | Ladder, boxed ladder (plated rails and gussets) or a spaceframe of tubes; on the side-by-side, gussets or extra chromoly bracing |

The nodes are named `Fit_<slot>_<part>[-<part>...]_<where>` with the slot
and part ids from `src/data/parts.ts` (for example `Fit_dampers_bypass_FL`).
`tests/models.test.ts` checks that every name is a real part for that class
and that every part you can see has its nodes, so a new part in
`parts.ts` turns the test red until the script draws it.

**Not drawn yet:** the light 4x4's wishbone front and trailing-arm rear, and
the side-by-side's long arms, still look like the stock layout (the light
4x4 shows them as a coil-sprung beam axle). Drawing them means moving the
shock mounts and the arms' pivots, which is its own job.

## How heavy it is for a phone

With the stock parts fitted, a truck is about 33,000 to 40,000 triangles and
about 160 draw calls; with the busiest parts, up to about 53,000 and 210.
Parts that aren't fitted aren't drawn at all. Wheels, tires and shocks are
made once and shared by the corners that use them. Plan PR 12's graphics
tiers can trim from there (shadows, pixel ratio, the smallest details).

## The moving parts

Every moving part is its own named node, so the game can move it:

| Node | What it is | What the game does with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Body` | Cab, frame, cage, everything on the springs | Bounces, pitches and rolls |
| `Hub_FL` (and FR, RL, RR) | Each wheel's upright | Moves up and down with its wheel |
| `Wheel_FL` … | Wheel and tire | Spins with the distance |
| `Axle_F`, `Axle_R` | Solid axle beams | Tilt as one wheel rises past the other, taking both wheels with them |
| `Arm_FL` … | Side-by-side arms | Swing from the body to follow the wheel |
| `ShockBody_FL`, `ShockShaft_FL`, `Spring_FL` … | The shock and its spring | Stretch between `MountTop_FL` (on the body) and `MountLow_FL` (on the hub) |

`src/render/truckModel.ts` loads a model and poses it from the ride sim's
numbers: body bounce, pitch and roll, and each wheel's height, all measured
from rest, exactly as `src/sim/ride.ts` keeps them.

## Ride height

The models are drawn at the stock ride height. A build that sags less
(stiffer springs, more preload) sits higher at that end; one that sags more
sits lower. `stanceOf` in `src/ui/truckView.ts` works it out from each
axle's `sag` (`src/sim/build.ts`), and the body tilts to match.

## Where you see it

- **Build screen:** your truck on the shop floor, turning slowly (not with
  reduced motion). Drag sideways to turn it; up and down still scrolls the
  page. It only draws while it's on screen.

- **The shop (the test track):** every rig run plays in 3D at the top of
  the live view, and the break replays in slow motion under the report's
  heading. See below.

## The test track

**The road is the sim's road.** Each rig's loop is a few pieces of ground
(`src/data/rigs.ts`), and the sim builds each piece's road from a seeded
recipe (`src/sim/ground.ts`): two lines of heights, one under each wheel
track. `src/render/terrain.ts` lays those very heights out as a gravel road:
the left track, a slight crown, the right track, then shoulders, a ditch, a
bank and the forest (spruce and birch, placed by a hash so they stay put).
Every ripple, whoop, rock and jump the truck reacts to is under its wheels.
Pieces join where both are flat (each piece's ground fades out at its ends).

**Clips, not every step.** The rigs run the sim far faster than real time
(a lap in 4 seconds), so the 3D can't show every step. Instead the lap
runner and the shaker keep a short pose log (`src/sim/poses.ts`): body
bounce, pitch and roll and each wheel's height, 50 times a simulated second,
the last 4 seconds in a ring. The log only reads the sim's state; a run
with it gives exactly the same result as one without (`tests/poses.test.ts`),
and it's off for the balance bot.

`src/ui/rigView.ts` keeps asking for the newest 2.5 seconds and plays them
at true speed (`src/render/trackView.ts`), cutting to the next of five
cameras (side, front, chase, wheel, high) for each clip, like a TV feed. The
shaker's beat is slowed so it never looks faster than 2.5 a second. Dust
trails the wheels, more the faster it goes.

**The break.** When a run ends on a break, the 3D view moves under the
report's heading (the canvas keeps its picture when it moves) and replays
the last 3 seconds at 0.3× speed, closing in on the corner that broke, then
shows the break and holds it before starting again:

| Failure | What you see |
| --- | --- |
| Damper fade | Smoke curling off the shock, thicker as the oil cooks |
| Bottomed out | A jolt and a burst of dust |
| Cracked mount | The shock's top end drops off its mount |
| Snapped axle | The wheel flops out at an angle |
| Cut tire | The wheel drops onto squashed rubber |
| Cracked frame | A jolt, the camera pulled in on the whole truck |

The pace of all this is in `VIEW_3D` in `src/data/rigs.ts`.

With reduced motion the camera stays side-on: no cuts, swoops or shakes
(the truck itself still moves as the sim says).

three.js and the models only load when a 3D view first comes near the
screen (`import('../render/garage')`), so the first screen stays quick; the
models folder doesn't count towards the first-load budget
(`scripts/check-bundle.mjs`). If the phone can't do WebGL, a line says so and
the rest of the game works as before.

The colours come from the page's CSS variables (`src/render/look.ts`), so
the 3D follows the roadbook look and turns to the night-stage colours in
dark mode.
