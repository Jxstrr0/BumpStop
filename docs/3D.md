# Bump Stop in 3D

Plan PR 5. How the trucks and the scenery are made and how the game moves
and plants them.

## The trucks are made by script

`scripts/blender/make-trucks.py` builds all three trucks with no Blender
window: run it with the Blender Python module (`pip install bpy==5.1.2`,
then `python scripts/blender/make-trucks.py`) or with Blender itself
(`blender --background --python scripts/blender/make-trucks.py`). Add
`-- sxs` (or `light4x4`, `heavy`) to build just one, and `full` or `lite`
for just that build of it. For each truck it writes two files to
`public/models/`: the full model, `truck-light4x4.glb.gz`, and a lighter one
for the Lite graphics tier, `truck-light4x4-lite.glb.gz` (see "Two builds"
below). They're ordinary `.glb` models, zipped. The files are committed; the
game's build never runs Blender. All six take about a minute and a half. Two
runs give files that differ a little (the baked shadows are sampled), so
rebuild and commit them only when the script changes.

**Why zipped:** the models are mostly long lists of numbers that repeat, so
zipping makes each one about a quarter of its size (about 600 to 850 KB to
download for a full model instead of 2.5 to 3.5 MB; 360 to 520 KB for a
lite one). The phone's browser unzips it itself (`DecompressionStream`, in
`src/render/glb.ts`), with no extra code to download.

Everything comes from one shared kit of parts, and each class has its own
body on top:

- **Light 4x4:** a lifted single-cab pickup. The shell is one curved skin
  from the grille to the back of the cab: a crowned bonnet, sides that lean
  in to the roof, sills that hang below the floor with real wheel arches cut
  up into them, a raked windscreen and side windows set into rubber seals,
  door shut lines, wipers. Round headlamps in chrome rings beside a slatted
  grille, a pressed-steel bumper with tow hooks, a one-piece bull bar with
  spot lamps, arch flares, a hollow bed with a ribbed floor, capped rails and
  a pressed tailgate, a sport bar with work lamps and a roof lamp bar.
- **Heavy truck:** a cab-over rally truck: a rounded cab with a split, raked
  windscreen and door windows set into seals, a sun visor and a roof
  deflector, a slatted grille, a deep bumper with the lamps let into it and
  magenta tow eyes, steps, big mirrors on hoops, mudguards over all four
  wheels, a service box with sunk-in roller shutters and lockers, back
  doors, a ladder, amber sand ladders, domed fuel tanks and mud flaps.
- **Side-by-side:** a tube cage whose sides are each one bent tube, two
  seats with headrests and magenta harnesses, half doors and window nets, a
  rounded nose with a magenta stripe and a tube bumper, black mudguards, a
  rear deck over the engine and a whip with a magenta flag.

No real brands, logos or people.

## The HD pass (v1.16)

Kelbo asked (27 November 2026) for the trucks and their parts to get the
same Blender pass the scenery had. What changed, and how it works:

- **Round things are round, and edges catch the light.** Tires have a
  proper carcass with tapered tread blocks and shoulder lugs; steel wheels
  are a pressed dish with real holes cut through it; every wheel has its
  nuts, a hub, a brake disc and a caliper; shocks have eyes, seals, caps
  and cans that hang clear of the spring; springs sit in seats. Each face
  corner gets its own shading direction (`soft_normals`): gentle edges
  blend, sharp ones stay crisp, and a flat panel stays flat beside its
  rounded edge.
- **The chassis is a chassis.** The light 4x4's frame rails kick up over
  the axles and have lightening holes, with a tower for each shock; the
  axles have a cast centre, swaged tubes and a bolted cover. **The links
  move:** the coil-sprung axles' radius arms, upper links and track bar are
  arms (`Arm_FL_Link`, `Arm_RL_UpperLink`, `Arm_F_Track`) that swing with
  the axle, and each leaf spring is two halves (`Arm_FL_LeafFore`,
  `Arm_FL_LeafAft`) pivoted at the hanger and the shackle, so the pack
  flattens and arches as the axle moves. No new game code: they're the same
  arms the wishbones use.
- **The bodies are skins, not boxes.** `Part.loft` skins a row of
  cross-sections into one shell; `Part.window` sets a patch of it back
  behind a rubber seal as glass; `Part.round_edges` rounds every hard edge
  like a fold in pressed steel; `Part.cut` cuts one solid out of another
  (the wheel arches, the bed, the holes in a wheel); `Part.sweep` bends one
  tube through a row of points (roll cages, bull bars, hoses).
- **One material, painted.** A phone pays for every material a mesh uses
  (one "draw call" each). So nearly the whole truck is one material,
  `Truck`, and what differs from part to part is painted on the mesh: the
  colour on each corner, and the finish (how rough, how metallic) as two
  numbers per corner that the material looks up in a four-dot picture
  inside the file. Only glass and the three kinds of lamp keep their own.
  A stock truck went from about 100 draws to under 60, while showing about
  three times the triangles. `PALETTE` and `FINISHES` at the top of the
  script name every colour.
- **Soft shadows are baked in.** Real parts are darker where they crowd
  together: inside a wheel, between tread blocks, under the body. Blender's
  Cycles renderer measures that once, at every corner of every mesh
  (`bake_shade`), and the answer is multiplied into the painted colours. It
  costs the phone nothing. Each part is shaded as it sits on a stock truck;
  wheels, shocks and springs, which the game turns and stretches, are
  shaded on their own.
- **Paint and metal reflect.** `truckEnvironment` in `src/render/look.ts`
  paints a tiny picture of a sky, a horizon and a sun, and the trucks'
  material mirrors it, so chrome, zinc and gloss paint look like themselves
  (before, bright metal could only be drawn as grey paint). It repaints
  itself for the night-stage colours. The scenery doesn't use it and looks
  as it did.

### Two builds

`truck-<class>-lite.glb.gz` is the same truck with the same parts and the
same node names (a test checks), with round things made of fewer sides and
the smallest details left off (bolts, ribs, tread sipes): `seg(n)` and
`if HD:` in the script. A tier with `liteModels` in `src/data/graphics.ts`
(Lite) loads it; the others load the full one. The tier is read when a
truck is loaded, so a truck on screen keeps the model it has.

Offline, the worker fetches the lite models up front and keeps a full one
the first time the game loads it (`src/pwa/precache.ts`, `src/pwa/sw.js`),
so a phone on Lite never downloads the big files; if a full model can't be
fetched, the lite one stands in.

## The driveline

Each truck has what turns its wheels, drawn slim and kept between the frame
rails so the shocks and springs stay in plain view (Kelbo asked, 27
November 2026: "a truck with only the suspension frame looks a little off"):

- **Light 4x4:** a straight-six leaning in the bay with its sump, oil
  filter, fan and radiator, a bellhousing and gearbox, a transfer case that
  drops to the left, a prop shaft to each axle, an exhaust down the right
  with a silencer, a fuel tank behind the rear axle and the body's mounts on
  the rails.
- **Heavy truck:** a diesel under the cab, a gearbox, a short shaft back to
  a transfer case in the middle of the frame, prop shafts both ways, cab
  mounts, bearers under the service box and air tanks.
- **Side-by-side:** the engine and gearcase at the back, a front diff, and
  one long prop shaft under the floor between them.

**The prop shafts move.** A solid axle goes up and down and the gearbox
doesn't, so each of those shafts is two halves that slide together like a
real slip joint: `Arm_F_Prop` is pivoted at the transfer case and points at
the axle's pinion, and `Arm_F_PropSlip` is pivoted on the axle and points
back at the transfer case (the same "arm" trick the wishbones use, so the
game needed no new code beyond knowing an arm can belong to an axle). On an
independent layout the diff is bolted to the frame, so its shaft is fixed.

The engine part still shows outside: the petrol six has its own tailpipe
now, the V8 splits into two, and the diesels run to their side exit. The
skid plate and the heavy truck's belly plate hang under the transfer case,
below the shafts.

Since v1.27 the driveline is always its own `Driveline` node on the body
(one more draw call per truck), so the X-ray view can take the body off and
keep the engine and gearbox. `tests/models.test.ts` checks it's there.

Each model carries every part a player can bolt on, and the game shows only
the build's own (`fit()` in `src/render/truckModel.ts`). Swap a part on the
build screen and the truck on the shop floor changes at once; the truck on
the test track and in the raids is the one you built.

| Slot | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Dampers | Twin-tube: plain black. Monotube: silver. Remote reservoir: blue end caps and a separate can on a hose. Bypass: a fat silver body with three outside tubes and blue adjusters. Internal bypass: black with a piggyback can. Electronic: black with an amber valve box and a wire. Zed-Two spool valve (2017): black with a square valve block under the top eye, three amber spool caps in a row and a piggyback can |
| Springs | Coil-sprung trucks show magenta coils and the axle's links; leaf-sprung ones show leaf packs (the top leaf is magenta) and no coils |
| Bump stops | Rubber: a black cone. Urethane: an amber stack. Hydraulic: a small silver can with a blue cap (gas-charged ones look the same) |
| Anti-roll bars | None, a plain black bar, or a bar with flat blue blade arms |
| Axles | Standard: a plain diff cover. Heavy-duty: a sleeved housing, a truss and a finned cover. Chromoly: a blue cover and chrome hub flanges. On the side-by-side: thicker drive shafts, or chrome shafts with blue boots |
| Wheels | Steel: a birch-white dish with round holes. Alloy: silver spokes. Beadlocks: black with a bright bolted ring (forged beadlocks look the same) |
| Tires | All-terrain: fine shallow blocks. Rally: big square blocks. Heavy ply: chunky lugs that climb a fat sidewall. Studded and aramid tires look like their plain twin. All the same size, so a swap never moves the truck |
| Guards | Skid plates under the nose and transfer case; full armour adds rock sliders, diff guards and tank guards |
| Spares | Nothing, one spare wheel and a crate, or two spares, more crates and red jerry cans |
| Engine | The bits you see outside: the petrol six's tailpipe, the V8's bonnet scoop and twin pipes, the diesels' snorkel, the turbo diesel's intercooler (the twin-turbo V6 shows the same); the heavy truck's exhaust stacks and air intake; the side-by-side's pipes, turbo and air box |
| Frame | Ladder, boxed ladder (plated rails and gussets) or a spaceframe of tubes; on the side-by-side, gussets or extra chromoly bracing |

The nodes are named `Fit_<slot>_<part>[-<part>...]_<where>` with the slot
and part ids from `src/data/parts.ts` (for example `Fit_dampers_bypass_FL`).
`tests/models.test.ts` checks that every name is a real part for that class
and that every part you can see has its nodes, so a new part in
`parts.ts` turns the test red until the script draws it. A new part that
looks like an old one can share its mesh: list it under the old part in
`TWINS` in the Blender script, and its name joins the old part's nodes
(`Fit_tires_rallyTires-studdedRally-aramidRally-studdedAramid_FL`).

## Suspension layouts

Some suspension parts change how the wheels hang, so the truck carries each
layout and shows the build's own:

| Truck | Part | What you see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light 4x4 | Solid axle (coil or leaf), front and rear | A beam between the wheels with the diff in the middle, on coil links or leaf packs. The beam tilts as one wheel rises past the other |
| Light 4x4 | Double wishbone (front) | A long lower arm from a crossmember under the frame and a shorter upper arm from brackets on the rail to a tall knuckle; the coilover sits on a bracket at the back of the lower arm, behind the upper arm, up to a shock tower. The diff is bolted to the frame, with a half-shaft out to each wheel and a tie rod from the steering rack |
| Light 4x4 | Trailing arm (rear) | A boxed arm on each side from a hanger under the rail back to the wheel, braced to a second bushing; the coilover stands on the arm, and the diff hangs from a crossmember with half-shafts |
| Side-by-side | Double wishbone (stock) | Upper and lower wishbones on a bracket plate, the long shock standing on the upright |
| Side-by-side | Long-travel arms | Pivots near the middle of the truck, so the arms are about half as long again and swing through a flatter arc: a boxed alloy lower arm with a chrome heim joint and a long alloy upper arm; the shock sits on the lower arm |
| Side-by-side | Trailing arm (stock) / long trailing arm | A trailing arm from ahead of the wheel and two radius rods; the long one is a deeper alloy arm reaching forward under the seats, braced to a second bushing, with longer rods and the shock on the arm |

The bump stops, anti-roll bars, diff covers (one per axle part), half-shafts
and full armour's diff guards follow the layout too.

**How it works.** Each layout's parts on the body sit in a group named like a
bolt-on, `Fit_frontSusp_wishbone_RigF` (the solid axle in
`Fit_frontSusp_solidAxleCoil-solidAxleLeaf_AxleF`), so `fit()` shows the
build's own and hides the rest. A hidden axle doesn't tilt the hubs. The
layouts that aren't stock have their own shock mounts, named after their
parts (`MountTop_FL_wishbone`, `MountLow_FL_wishbone`); the script places
them so the shock is exactly as long at rest as the stock one, so the same
shock bodies and springs fit every layout, and `fit()` hangs each shock on
the build's mounts. Some bottom mounts sit on an arm rather than the hub, so
the shock moves less than the wheel, like a real one. `tests/models.test.ts`
checks the lengths match and that every arm has its end.

## Mud, dust, snow and dents (v1.20)

Damage you can see (Kelbo picked it, 27 November 2026). Over a raid the
truck gets dusty, muddy and snowy, the paint chips low down, and dents show
where things went wrong. It's only a look: nothing in the sim, the balance
or saves reads it.

**How much** comes from what the raid really did (`src/sim/grime.ts`, every
number in `src/data/grime.ts`), worked out fresh each clip from what the
raid already keeps, so nothing new is saved:

- each km of ground adds a little (dust on dry summer gravel, mud on rough
  ground and whoops, snow on snow and frost heaves, grey slush on frozen
  gravel), more at Push and less at Conserve;
- a thaw turns it to shiny fresh mud, a dust storm coats it, a whiteout
  plasters it, from where the weather turned;
- digging out of a snowbank packs snow in, a cracked ice road splashes slush;
- the night at the bivouac knocks off the worst of the mud and melts the
  snow (the dust stays in the seams); the rest day washes more; a new raid
  starts clean;
- rocks the guards took chip the paint; a snowbank dents the front corner, a
  corner that bottomed, tore a mount, snapped an axle or cut a tire knocks
  its wheel arch about (body dents stay for the raid); a rock that got past
  the guards dents the oil pan, diff or tank until the crew patches it.

**How it's drawn** (`src/render/grime.ts`): the truck's shared `Truck`
material gets a grimy copy per truck with a few lines added to three.js's
shader, so there are no textures and no extra draw calls. For each pixel it
works out the height above the ground, the nearness of a wheel arch and the
way the surface faces, in the body's own space (so the mud line stays put as
the body bounces), and a splotchy noise pattern breaks the edges up. The
wheels get a second copy whose pattern is in their own space, so it turns
with them. Dents bend the surface shading (like a bump map) with scraped
paint and a crease round the edge. Shocks and springs keep the clean paint,
so the suspension stays easy to see. The Lite tier (`grimeDetail` off in
`src/data/graphics.ts`) draws one layer of pattern, no chips, and dents as
scraped paint only.

The raid screen hands each clip its grime (`raidGrime(state, run, at)`) via
`RaidView.show` and `TrackView.setGrime`; the test track and the garage
show a clean truck. The truck harness takes `grime=` (a `Grime` as JSON) to
check the look.

Once a night stage gets dark the stage view turns to night (moonlight, close

## How heavy it is for a phone

With the stock parts fitted, a full truck shows about 52,000 to 62,000
triangles in 50 to 60 draw calls, and a lite one about 28,000 to 34,000 in
the same number of draws. At Full and Ultra the shadow pass draws it a
second time; Lite has no shadows. (Before the HD pass a truck was about
20,000 triangles in about 100 draws: far fewer triangles, but draws are
what a phone feels first.) About half the triangles are the four wheels and
tires.
Parts that aren't fitted aren't drawn at all. Wheels, tires and shocks are
made once and shared by the corners that use them.

## The moving parts

Every moving part is its own named node, so the game can move it:

| Node | What it is | What the game does with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Body` | Cab, frame, cage, everything on the springs | Bounces, pitches and rolls |
| `Hub_FL` (and FR, RL, RR) | Each wheel's upright | Moves up and down with its wheel |
| `Wheel_FL` … | Wheel and tire | Spins with the distance |
| `Axle_F`, `Axle_R` | Solid axle beams | Tilt as one wheel rises past the other, taking both wheels with them |
| `Arm_FL_Lower`, `Arm_FL_Upper`, `Arm_FL_Shaft` … | Wishbones, trailing arms, radius rods, tie rods and half-shafts | Each turns from its pivot on the body to point at its `ArmEnd_FL_…` on the hub, so it follows the wheel |
| `Arm_FL_Link`, `Arm_RL_UpperLink`, `Arm_F_Track`, `Arm_FL_LeafFore`, `Arm_FL_LeafAft` … | A solid axle's links, its track bar, and the two halves of each leaf spring | The same: each turns at its bracket, hanger or shackle on the body to point at its end on the axle |
| `Arm_F_Prop`, `Arm_F_PropSlip` (and R) | The two halves of a prop shaft to a solid axle | One turns at the transfer case to point at `ArmEnd_F_Prop` on the axle; the other turns on the axle to point back at `ArmEnd_F_PropSlip` on the body, so they slide together |
| `ShockBody_FL`, `ShockShaft_FL`, `Spring_FL` … | The shock and its spring | Stretch between `MountTop_FL` (on the body) and `MountLow_FL` (on the hub or an arm), or the layout's own mounts |

`src/render/truckModel.ts` loads a model and poses it from the ride sim's
numbers: body bounce, pitch and roll, and each wheel's height, all measured
from rest, exactly as `src/sim/ride.ts` keeps them.

## Ride height

The models are drawn at the stock ride height. A build that sags less
(stiffer springs, more preload) sits higher at that end; one that sags more
sits lower. `stanceOf` in `src/ui/truckView.ts` works it out from each
axle's `sag` (`src/sim/build.ts`), and the body tilts to match.

## Truck, X-ray or Both (v1.27)

Kelbo asked (27 November 2026) for a three-way switch on the 3D views. A
strip of three buttons sits under the build screen's truck, over the test
track and the raid stage (by Photo mode), and in photo mode's bar:

- **Truck:** as built.
- **X-ray:** the body comes off: `BodyShell` (cab, bed, panels, glass and
  lamps) and the bolt-ons on it (`SEE_THROUGH.bodySlots` in
  `src/data/graphics.ts`: light bar, spots, spares, recovery kit). The frame,
  the `Driveline`, guards, bars, bump stops, axles, arms, shocks and wheels
  stay.
- **Both:** the body stays as a see-through skin (`SEE_THROUGH.opacity`,
  glass fainter), with no shadow so the sun still lights what's under it.

`TruckModel.setLook` does it. X-ray hides nodes (and `fit()` keeps a hidden
bolt-on hidden); Both gives this copy its own see-through clones of the
body's materials, so the shared ones and other trucks are left alone. The
grimy paint goes on the solid body (`grimeOn` switches to Truck while it
swaps materials, then back), and the see-through skin is clean. A ghost truck
only loses its body in X-ray; it's see-through already. The truck card's
snapshot always shows the truck as built.

It's one pick for every view: `src/ui/truckLook.ts` keeps it (localStorage
`bumpstop.truckLook`) and tells every view and every strip when it changes.
Lite works the same (the lite models have the same nodes).

## The test track

**The road is the sim's road.** Each rig's loop is a few pieces of ground
(`src/data/rigs.ts`), and the sim builds each piece's road from a seeded
recipe (`src/sim/ground.ts`): two lines of heights, one under each wheel
track. `src/render/terrain.ts` lays those very heights out as a gravel road:
the left track, a slight crown, the right track, then shoulders, a ditch and
a grassy verge. Every ripple, whoop, rock and jump the truck reacts to is
under its wheels. Pieces join where both are flat (each piece's ground fades
out at its ends). Past the clip the road carries on in coarser rows, so it
runs off into the haze.

**Birchland round it** (the scenery pass, v1.15). The rule that shapes all
of it: **the cameras stand on the truck's left, and nothing tall stands
between them and the truck.** The suspension is the show.

- **The land** (`terrain.ts`): low and open on the left (heath, grass, a
  lake now and then), climbing into forested hills on the right, behind the
  truck. It's a coarse mesh that meets the fine road strip along a seam 7 m
  out. Heights and colours come only from where you are along and across
  the road, so the land stays put, and a rig's loop has the same land every
  lap. Ice and snow stages get the same land under snow, with frozen lakes
  and a white spray off the wheels.
- **The scenery** (`scenery.ts`): spruce, pine and birch, rocks, heather,
  fireweed, cotton grass and reeds, planted from the tables in
  `src/data/scenery.ts` (`SUMMER`, `WINTER`); then what people put there:
  marker posts, a roadbook caution board (!, !!, !!!) before washboard,
  whoops, jumps and rocks, a power line, dry-stone walls with a gate, a
  stone bothy, a cairn. A rig's loop also has a start line (a chequered
  arch, a timing hut, tire stacks, pennants, straw bales); a raid stage
  doesn't. Each kind of piece is drawn in one go however many there are.
- **The sky** (`sky.ts`): a gradient from the weather's haze up to the
  colour overhead, the sun, three rings of far hills, a few clouds, and on a
  clear night stage the stars and the northern lights. Thaw, whiteout and
  dust close it in to the haze alone, as before.
- **The shaker rig** stands in the team's workshop: block and tin walls,
  high windows, a roller door, a tire rack, a bench, a tool chest, the
  rig's console, and four hydraulic posts whose pans ride up and down under
  the wheels.

**The scenery is made by script too.** `scripts/blender/make-scenery.py`
(run it like the truck script) writes `public/models/scenery.glb.gz`: about
55 small pieces, one mesh each, with their colours painted on the corners
and no textures. Running it twice gives the same file. To add a piece, write
its builder there, add it to `build_all`, and name it in a table in
`src/data/scenery.ts`; `tests/scenery.test.ts` checks every name in the
tables is in the file, that nothing tall is planted on the cameras' side,
and that a lap later the trees are the same trees.

**What it costs.** At Full the scenery is about 100,000 triangles in about
35 draw calls (the road strip is about 45,000 more, as before). The graphics
tiers plant a share of the tables (`cover` in `src/data/graphics.ts`): Lite
0.3 (and no walls), Full 0.85, Ultra 1.3. The far hillsides use a
72-triangle spruce.

**Clips, not every step.** The rigs run the sim far faster than real time
(a lap in 4 seconds), so the 3D can't show every step. Instead the lap
runner and the shaker keep a short pose log (`src/sim/poses.ts`): body
bounce, pitch and roll and each wheel's height, 50 times a simulated second,
the last 4 seconds in a ring. The log only reads the sim's state; a run
with it gives exactly the same result as one without (`tests/poses.test.ts`),
and it's off for the balance bot.

`src/ui/rigView.ts` keeps asking for the newest 2.5 seconds and plays them
at true speed (`src/render/trackView.ts`), cutting to the next of five
cameras (side, front, chase, wheel, high) for each clip, like a TV feed. The
shaker's beat is slowed so it never looks faster than 2.5 a second. Dust
trails the wheels, more the faster it goes.

**Full page.** A rig run takes the whole page (Kelbo asked, 27 November
2026): the 3D view fills the screen above the tabs, and the numbers sit
round the edges on slips of paper so the middle stays clear for the truck.
The lap line, the lap's progress and the frame's wear run along the top;
the front corners sit under them and the rear corners at the bottom (down
the two sides on a wide screen), each with its oil temperature and band
word, travel and wear; Fast, Skip and Stop run along the bottom. **Traces**
(top right) drops back to the page, with the 3D view in a box and the live
trace under it, and **Full page** goes back. The phone remembers which
(`bumpstop.trackFull`, `src/ui/fullPage.ts`). When the run ends the replay
keeps the screen, with the report's headline and **See the report** and
**Run it again** along the bottom. If the phone can't do WebGL the run stays
in the page. The layout is all CSS (`.live.full` in `src/style.css`); the
shop only switches the class (`layout` in `src/ui/shop.ts`).

**A raid stage is full page too** (Kelbo asked the same day): the highlight
fills the screen above the tabs, with the leg and the stage's progress along
the top, two gauges under it (the hottest oil and the most worn part, each
with its band word, since there's no chart to read them off), the last two
lines of the log (a stop, the ice creaking, the weather turning) and the
skip button along the bottom. At a checkpoint the card comes up from the
bottom with the pace call and **Drive on**; the gauges step aside for it
because it says the same things. **Traces** and **Full page** swap as on the
test track, and it's the same remembered pick. The stage shares `.live.full`
with the test track; `.raid-live` in `src/style.css` holds what's different
(`stageScreen` in `src/ui/raid.ts`).

The cameras stand where they always did, so a canvas of another shape
changes the lens, not the camera (`src/render/framing.ts`, numbers in
`TRACK_CAMERA` in `src/data/graphics.ts`): the shots were framed for 16:10
at 40° top to bottom, and on a taller canvas the lens opens up to keep the
picture as wide, up to 72° on a phone held upright (the truck then fills
about half the width). A very big canvas (a computer window) is drawn a
little softer so it stays smooth: each tier has a pixel budget (`maxPixels`)
that a phone screen fits under.

**The swing-around camera** (v1.19, Kelbo picked it, 27 November 2026).
Every run and stage starts on the TV-style shots above, so the suspension
is easy to see. Drag the picture and the camera is yours: it swings round
the truck (sideways drags turn it, up and down lifts it) and keeps
following the truck from there, clip after clip, until **Auto camera**
gives it back to the shots. Two fingers pinch to zoom; on a computer the
mouse drags and the wheel zooms (full page; in the page the wheel scrolls
and a trackpad pinch zooms). With the picture focused the arrow keys swing
it, + and − zoom and Escape goes back to the auto camera. Full page any
drag on the picture swings it, and the numbers and buttons over it still
take their own taps; in the page an up-and-down drag still scrolls the page,
so only sideways drags swing it there. A slip says how ("Drag to swing the
camera round; pinch to zoom") until the first try; the phone remembers
(`bumpstop.swingTried`). The player's camera stays through checkpoints,
stages and runs until they tap **Auto camera**. On a break replay it keeps
the player's angle but looks more and more at the corner that broke.

It never goes where the scenery would block it: the camera comes in closer
rather than go past 7 m out on the right (the forest starts at 8.5 m) or
past the cameras' clear lane on the left, and in the workshop it stays
inside the walls and under the roof. Photo mode uses the same limits.
Numbers in `SWING` in `src/data/graphics.ts`; the camera is
`swingStart`/`swingSet`/`swingEnd` and `orbit` in
`src/render/trackView.ts`; the drag and pinch are shared with photo mode in
`src/ui/orbitDrag.ts`; the button, slip and keys are `src/ui/swingCamera.ts`.

**The break.** When a run ends on a break, the 3D view replays the last 3
seconds at 0.3× speed, closing in on the corner that broke, then shows the
break and holds it before starting again. Full page it has the whole
screen; in the page (and after **See the report**) it moves under the
report's heading (the canvas keeps its picture when it moves):

| Failure | What you see |
| --- | --- |
| Damper fade | Smoke curling off the shock, thicker as the oil cooks |
| Bottomed out | A jolt and a burst of dust |
| Cracked mount | The shock's top end drops off its mount |
| Snapped axle | The wheel flops out at an angle |
| Cut tire | The wheel drops onto squashed rubber |
| Cracked frame | A jolt, the camera pulled in on the whole truck |

**The finish (v1.26).** When a run lasts every lap, the truck doesn't just
freeze short of the line: it drives through the start-line arch at full
speed, brakes at about 0.7 g (the nose dips onto the front springs) and
pulls up, then rocks back on its springs. On the jump ramp it holds its
speed over the tabletop and only brakes once it has landed. A racing ghost
that lasted too rolls out beside it. The camera for this stands on the
cameras' side of the road, four truck lengths ahead, looking back, and
eases to a stop just past where the truck pulls up, so the run ends on the
truck from the front. With reduced motion it stays on (it only turns to
follow). The roll-out is worked out after the run on a copy of the truck's
state (`rollOut` in `src/sim/laps.ts`), so the result never changes; a
stopped run or a broken one shows its last clip or the break as before.
The numbers are `ROLL_OUT` in `src/data/rigs.ts`. Raid stages have no
arch, so their last clip is unchanged.

The pace of all this is in `VIEW_3D` in `src/data/rigs.ts`.

With reduced motion the camera stays side-on: no cuts, swoops or shakes
(the truck itself still moves as the sim says).

three.js and the models only load when a 3D view first comes near the
screen (`import('../render/garage')`), so the first screen stays quick; the
models folder doesn't count towards the first-load budget
(`scripts/check-bundle.mjs`). If the phone can't do WebGL, a line says so and
the rest of the game works as before.

The colours come from the page's CSS variables (`src/render/look.ts`), so
the 3D follows the roadbook look and turns to the night-stage colours in
dark mode.
